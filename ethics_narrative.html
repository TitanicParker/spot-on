Governed ethics narrative

The Unresolved Relationship

This is not principally a story about whether Parkinsonism existed. It is a story about what happened to a relationship that remained clinically material: painful and structurally abnormal feet on one side, a directly observed dystonic-looking lower limb and severe young-onset Parkinsonism on the other.

Governing principle. An explanation cannot legitimately substitute for the evidence that would test the explanation when testing remains reasonably available, clinically warranted and materially relevant. The ethical question is therefore not whether the eventual explanations were plausible. It is whether they were allowed to perform the work of investigation without being exposed to the inquiry capable of testing them.

I. The Case Did Not Enter Neurology Pre-Separated

In April 2017 the primary-care problem already joined bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait. By June the GP was discussing gait, feet and “muscle tension” together. Increased tone, brisk reflexes, initiation difficulty, toe clawing, declining dexterity and worsening function accumulated until Neurology received an urgent referral for an unusual, stiff, festinant gait with bilateral foot issues and possible Parkinsonism.

The ethical starting point is therefore an integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem. Parkinsonism was one possible explanation inside that problem; it was not yet a permission to treat the feet as a separate settled object.

Structural foot disease was real. The record documented mallet/clawed toes, callus and pressure pathology, and later pes planus and hammertoes. Podiatry was already a legitimate destination. None of that established neurological independence.

During the July admission Neurology then supplied the most important competing observation itself. The gait was “strikingly abnormal”; the lower-limb appearance resembled foot drop but was described as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.” Severe rigidity and bradykinesia were present in the same information state.

The unresolved object was visible in 2017:

painful / structurally abnormal feet ↔ dystonic-looking lower-limb movement.

The patient also supplied evidence favouring distinction. He described foot pain reducing after leaving bar work while leg tension continued, and came to see them as “not the same problem.” That is important phenomenology. But a patient's ability to distinguish two experiences does not establish that their biological mechanisms are independent.

Phenomenological distinction had evidence. Biological independence remained an inference.

II. The Discharge Note Did More Than Summarise

The July discharge synthesis was not unsophisticated. It was unusually sophisticated. It considered very-young-onset Parkinsonism, atypical phenotype, recessive inheritance, PRKN/PARK2, PARK6, PARK7, PARK9, imaging, prognosis, genetics, treatment sequencing and the risks of early dopaminergic therapy. It expressly acknowledged, “I am not a movement disorders neurologist.”

That sophistication makes the ethical problem harder, not easier.

The question is not why sophisticated thinking was unavailable. It is why extraordinary sophistication was brought to bear on disease subtype, genetics, prognosis and treatment while the concrete relationship between painful feet and a dystonic-looking limb did not receive equivalent relational development.

More importantly, the note itself organised the facts. One branch became:

Parkinsonism
↓
very young onset · atypical phenotype · recessive genetics
↓
PRKN/PARK2 · PARK6 · PARK7 · PARK9
↓
imaging · prognosis · treatment strategy

The foot territory acquired another route:

corns / calluses / foot pain + “burning”
↓
“leg symptoms”
↓
“may have been completely separate”
↓
“perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy” · possible alcohol contribution
↓
Podiatry / separate future

Every link in that second route could be medically discussable. The ethical question concerns its function. A sensory descriptor first visible in the disclosed record within the compact neuropathy synthesis helped create neuropathy salience. Heterogeneous facts were bundled into “leg symptoms.” The patient's experiential distinction was strengthened into language of being “markedly divorced.” Alcohol, originally recorded in the context of attempted muscle relaxation, became available as a possible cause.

The note therefore became a constitutive epistemic act. It did not simply report what was known. It assigned observations to categories, categories to possible causes, and causes to future owners.

The critical ethical distinction

A plausible hypothesis can be a reason when it generates, follows or remains answerable to proportionate investigation. The same hypothesis can become a rationalisation when it acquires force after challenge, permits the existing disposition to survive, and is not exposed to the evidence capable of distinguishing it from alternatives.

The words alone cannot tell us which occurred. Chronology and conduct must.

III. The Protest Is the Temporal Hinge

The Protest is not treated here as contemporaneously corroborated fact merely because it is important. In the retained public record it is later patient evidence. The stronger analysis therefore proceeds conditionally: if the Protest occurred as later described, it changes the ethical character of what followed.

The patient's reported objection was simple. He did not claim to know PRKN or dystonia. He said, in substance, that the disabling bilateral foot problem had not been addressed before discharge. His later account preserves the analogy: would the hospital give him the “full NCT” and send him out on “bald tires”?

That is an objection to completion, not a retrospective diagnosis.

If the responsible service received that objection while the patient, the dystonic-looking sign and the unfinished documentary product were still available, a genuine choice-space existed. Re-examination, movement-disorders input, a neurological question to Podiatry, explicit preservation of uncertainty, a defined return condition, or a documented decision to proceed were all conceptually available. Prevention did not require solving the whole case before discharge.

The ethical hinge is therefore this:

Did the challenge reopen the patient, or did it mainly reopen the explanation?

The note remained capable of receiving consequential information after physical discharge: the DaTscan result was incorporated into the still-open discharge product. That does not prove a further bedside examination was practical. It does show that documentary closure and physical discharge were not the same moment.

On the concerning hypothesis, the record produced after the challenge was capable of defending the disposition rather than preserving the warning.

IV. The Patient and the Decision Form One Epistemic Mechanism

The deepest ethical problem is not that the decision became privileged while the patient separately became less credible. Those are the same operation.

A decision becomes resistant to correction only if contrary evidence can be made less capable of changing it. In this case the most movable object was the meaning of the patient's experience.

When the patient's statements supported separation, they could be incorporated into the architecture: pain had reduced; tension remained; he had “in his own mind — markedly divorced” the phenomena. When his account threatened the adequacy of closure, the alleged Protest did not travel in the authoritative account available to later readers.

The patient thus risks being transformed from a participant capable of changing the model into material that the model is entitled to interpret.

One mechanism: decision fixed; patient made flexible.

Pain can be reclassified. Chronology can be redescribed. Treatment response can be described as nonspecific modulation. A later diagnosis can update present care while leaving the historical decision untouched. Each move may be defensible in isolation. Together they reveal the ethical structure to be tested.

The system preserves the epistemic status of the decision by reducing the epistemic force of the contradiction.

That does not require dishonesty. It can occur through selection, arrangement, omission, unequal preservation and institutional inheritance.

V. The Later Clinic Notes Are Stress Tests, Not Independent Confirmations

Once an authoritative specialist synthesis exists, later clinicians do not encounter only a patient. They encounter a patient plus the accumulated record. That creates path dependence. Repetition of an inherited possibility is not the same as independent re-demonstration.

2018: treatment state enters the disputed territory

During withdrawal of procyclidine, the outside-left-foot problem was among the first phenomena reported to change rapidly as movement and tension worsened. Restoration of treatment improved the overall state. This does not prove that a drug created or removed a corn. It is an anomaly: the disputed foot territory was behaving partly with neurological treatment state.

2020: apparent foot drop is reclassified internally

Neurology later described the lower-limb “throw” as looking like foot drop but being “actually parkinsonian” because dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power were normal. This is important internal recognition in the same territory. Yet it functions as a local patch unless it reopens the founding relationship.

2023: the model comes closest to reopening

Severe bilateral foot pain, cramping and walking difficulty prompted explicit consideration of Parkinsonian dystonia. The cramping was judged more likely an OFF dystonia; later leg pain and shuffling were treated as OFF-related. The neurologist also acknowledged a “misalignment”: the patient had reasonable concerns that much related to the feet as a separate problem while the clinician might appear to explain everything by Parkinson's.

This is genuine clinical reflection. But the deeper question is whether the founding allocation itself changed. The new material could be assimilated as OFF dystonia, pain magnification and a continuing separate foot problem without reconstructing the original join.

2024–2025: pharmacology increases pressure

Pregabalin helped much of the pain but did not remove toe curling/postural phenomena. In 2025 the patient described the first steps as “torture” and reported relief after Parkinson medication reached effect. Sinemet CR was later described in the complaint response as producing “profound relief.”

The treating neurologist offered a plausible alternative interpretation: dopaminergic treatment can alter pain experience regardless of the underlying cause. Again the central ethical question is not whether that explanation is possible. It is whether a potentially falsifying observation was converted into another proposition under which the founding partition could survive without being globally retested.

Local adaptability is not the same as global corrigibility.

The system could change medication, symptom attribution, dose timing and local explanations while leaving the epistemic status of the original partition intact.

VI. The Complaint Response Changes the Function of the Record

In March 2025 the patient formally alleged diagnostic anchoring, alcohol-neuropathy framing and failure to reassess. The treating neurologist responded by emphasising the breadth of prior investigation, the tentative nature of neuropathy, the reasons for cautious levodopa use, the willingness to consider alternative diagnoses and the many adjustments made over time.

Those are relevant answers to some accusations. But under the governing framework there is a category distinction:

“I changed many things over the years” does not by itself answer “Did the particular relationship separated in 2017 ever undergo model-level reconstruction?”

The complaint response therefore has a new documentary function. The longitudinal record is no longer only a record of care. It is recruited as evidence that the clinician was epistemically open.

The record becomes advocacy-capable: later adaptations are used to defend the original architecture against the charge of anchoring.

This does not make the response improper. It makes evidential independence crucial. The same accumulated record cannot automatically serve as independent proof of the adequacy of the model that organised it.

VII. Governance Is the Next Recursive Layer

The later institutional process concluded that there had been no deficit in care. The ethical question is not whether governance was entitled to reach that conclusion. It is how independently the conclusion tested the founding problem.

If governance relies substantially on the accumulated clinical record and the original clinician's explanatory account without independently reconstructing the 2017 feet–movement relationship, its conclusion is not an independent repetition of the clinical evidence. It is a second-order validation of a record generated under the model being challenged.

2017 decision
↓
2017 explanatory record
↓
later care interpreted through inherited record
↓
later record used in clinician defence
↓
governance reviews accumulated record
↓
institutional validation strengthens authority of founding account

This is recursive validation. Each layer can look more independent while remaining epistemically dependent on the original frame.

Data governance then adds another form of stability. The Article 16 refusal distinguishes whether an opinion was medically correct from whether the historical record accurately records that the opinion was held. That is a legitimate distinction. But it changes the truth condition being tested:

  • Is the account correct enough to guide care?
  • Was the account professionally reasonable?
  • Does the record accurately preserve the historical opinion?

A medically contestable opinion can still be an accurate record of a historical opinion. The original architecture can therefore move from clinical hypothesis to travelling specialist account to protected historical record without the protection itself demonstrating the hypothesis true.

VIII. January–February 2026: Later Truth Meets Historical Insulation

In January 2026 QPS stated that a subsequent December 2025 diagnosis of dystonia did not undermine the validity of the prior complaint processes or outcomes, did not require reopening governance reviews, did not require alteration of prior determinations, and did not require retrospective amendment of historical records.

That is ethically important because it is a meta-rule about what later evidence is allowed to do.

A later diagnosis does not automatically prove earlier negligence. Nor should every changed diagnosis reopen every historical process. But in this case the later evidence arose in the exact territory that had been disputed from the beginning. Treating it as categorically unable to interrogate the prior outcome risks historical insulation: present clinical truth may update while historical accountability remains fixed.

In February 2026 an external specialist then recorded that genetic testing confirmed two pathogenic Parkin variants and that the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia were due to compound heterozygous PRKN mutations.

This does not prove what clinicians should have known molecularly in July 2017. It does provide a powerful retrospective stress test: the dystonic component observed in the founding admission belonged centrally to the same monogenic disease process as the Parkinsonism.

The endpoint of self-sealing

If later evidence can change treatment and present diagnosis but, by institutional rule, cannot alter the epistemic status of the earlier decision or the governance conclusion built around it, the system can learn clinically while refusing to learn historically.

IX. The Ethical Finding Is About Corrigibility

The strongest ethical theory is not “they should have diagnosed dystonia in 2017.” It is not “peripheral neuropathy was impossible.” It is not that every later foot symptom must have one cause.

The ethical issue is whether a materially unresolved relationship was converted into a sufficiently plausible separate future without proportionate investigation capable of testing that separation, and whether later contradictory evidence was repeatedly assimilated without altering the epistemic status of the founding decision.

This is why “anchoring” is too small a concept. Anchoring names a cognitive tendency. The deeper concern is epistemic self-sealing: an architecture in which contrary evidence can always be accommodated while the model itself remains unavailable for revision.

A functioning clinical system should preserve a route by which error can generate correction. That route depends on:

  • distinguishing observation from interpretation;
  • preserving uncertainty where the relationship remains untested;
  • allowing the patient to supply model-changing information rather than merely interpretable data;
  • requiring explanations to remain answerable to proportionate testing;
  • preventing inherited records from masquerading as independent corroboration;
  • and allowing later material evidence to interrogate earlier institutional conclusions where it bears directly on the disputed relationship.

The core asymmetry can be stated simply:

The system could change almost everything except the epistemic status of the original decision because it could reinterpret almost everything about the patient instead.

That is not two failures. It is one mechanism.

The patient is the principal carrier of contrary reality. If the decision is fixed, the meaning of the patient's experience must remain flexible enough to absorb the contradiction.

The ethical question is therefore not whether sophisticated clinicians could construct a plausible explanation.

It is whether expertise remained answerable to the evidence capable of changing the decision — or whether explanatory sophistication progressively made the decision less answerable to that evidence.

Evidential Limits That Govern This Narrative

This narrative must remain falsifiable by the evidence it invokes.

  • The public repository contains a scoped retained record, not the full master corpus.
  • Documentary absence is stated as “not visible” or “not demonstrated,” not “never happened.”
  • The Protest is later patient evidence unless independently corroborated.
  • Later PRKN genetics cannot be projected backwards as knowledge available in 2017.
  • “Burning” first becomes visible in the disclosed record within the neuropathy synthesis; that does not establish fabrication.
  • Peripheral neuropathy remained medically possible; the argument concerns its evidential and functional status.
  • Podiatry was appropriate for genuine structural foot disease; referral destination does not itself establish biological separation.
  • The visible retained Podiatry form does not presently support a categorical claim that “No neuropathy” was ticked.
  • The stronger hypothesis of anticipatory defensive documentation requires chronology and provenance. Legitimate comprehensive discharge documentation remains a live innocent explanation.
  • Clinical negligence, professional misconduct, causation and dishonest intent require evidence beyond this ethical narrative.

If native audit history shows the consequential separation passages predated the alleged Protest and arose from identifiable clinical evidence, the post-notice defensive hypothesis narrows. If independent expert review demonstrates that the relationship was proportionately examined and reasonably left separate, the central allegation narrows further. The repository's governing principle applies to its own theory as much as to the clinical model it criticises.
