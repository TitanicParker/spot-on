The Legal Shape of the Case

Duty, breach, harm, causation, proof and prima facie structure — organised around the pre-closure phase change rather than a simple delayed-diagnosis narrative.

For personal understanding only. Not legal advice. Breach, causation and standard of care require appropriately instructed independent expert evidence.
Contents

1. What this page is — and is not

This is a legal-facing analytical map for personal understanding, not legal advice and not a finding of negligence. It translates the evidential architecture into the ordinary components that a negligence case would eventually have to prove: fact, duty, breach, harm, causation and proof.

The distinctive feature of this case is the phase change at the 2017 discharge synthesis. The central legal question is therefore not simply whether later care was reasonable. It is whether a material unresolved relationship was adequately handled before an authoritative travelling record crystallised it, and what consequences followed if it was not.

Independent Irish legal advice and appropriate expert neurological evidence remain essential before any legal conclusion can be drawn.

2. The hierarchy: fact → proposition → inference → case theory

A useful discipline is to keep four levels separate.

Level Example
Fact Neurology documented severe Parkinsonism and a dystonic-looking lower-limb posture.
Proposition Structural foot pathology and neurological movement may have been partly independent.
Inference The final synthesis gave the separate/peripheral explanation greater documentary authority than the preceding record alone had established.
Case theory After direct pre-closure notice, a still-contestable relationship may have been prematurely converted into a settled travelling information state and later inherited without sufficient reconstruction.

A legal case fails if these levels are silently collapsed. A plausible case theory is not proof of breach. Later genetics is not proof of what clinicians should have known in 2017. A missing note is not proof an act never occurred.

3. Duty

At the highest level, the clinical relationship plainly involved duties of competent specialist assessment, treatment, communication and record-making. The harder question is what those duties required in the specific 2017 circumstances.

Given the information actually available in 2017—including painful and structurally abnormal feet, escalating movement abnormalities, toe clawing, severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a directly observed dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon, and the pre-discharge corrective notice—what proportionate clinical steps would ordinarily competent specialist care have required before or as a condition of closure?

Possible answers could include further examination, specialist input, a mixed-mechanism formulation, explicit preservation of uncertainty, a defined review pathway, or a documented reason why no additional inpatient inquiry was required.

The documentary analysis cannot answer that standard-of-care question by itself.

4. Breach: the candidate mechanism

The strongest breach theory is not “failure to diagnose dystonia.” It is a process theory.

A possible breach would arise if:

  1. a clinically material relationship remained unresolved;
  2. the responsible clinician received direct notice before closure that the originating problem remained inadequately addressed;
  3. proportionate corrective options remained available;
  4. no adequate post-notice reassessment, preservation of uncertainty or reasoned closure occurred;
  5. yet the final synthesis gave the separate/peripheral allocation a degree of authority and transmissibility that the underlying inquiry had not earned.

That is premature documentary closure as a candidate breach mechanism.

The theory can be defeated by evidence that the relationship was adequately considered, that the Protest concerned a different issue, that the separation was already clinically established, or that expert evidence shows no further inquiry was reasonably required.

5. The phase change matters to breach

Before the discharge synthesis, the relevant evidence is comparatively independent of the final model. After the synthesis, later clinicians inherit a specialist account that already contains categories and causal possibilities. This means downstream consistency cannot be counted mechanically as independent corroboration of the founding allocation.

The adequacy of the pre-closure process must be tested primarily from the pre-closure and immediate post-notice state, not retrospectively authenticated by the coherence of records generated after the model had begun to govern the pathway.

This does not make later records irrelevant. They are highly relevant as evidence of harm, management, treatment response and whether the original model was ever reopened.

6. Why the levodopa defence is not the whole breach question

The 2017 record appears unusually careful about why levodopa was being delayed in a very young patient. That creates a strong answer to a later allegation that treatment should simply have begun earlier.

But the core breach theory is not primarily a levodopa theory.

  • Treatment-timing question: Was caution about levodopa reasonable?
  • Founding-process question: What happened after the patient directly said that the problem which brought him into care remained inadequately addressed?

A strong answer to the first does not automatically answer the second. This is why the later complaint response and governance process must be examined for question migration.

7. Record integrity as part of the factual mechanism

A medical record can be literally composed of plausible sentences and still alter the apparent epistemic state of a case through selection, categorisation and omission.

The relevant questions are what was directly observed; what was merely hypothesised; what had actually been tested; what remained uncertain; what the patient disputed; and what future clinicians were expected to inherit.

If separation-supporting patient statements were preserved while a materially contrary pre-closure objection was not, that asymmetry can be evidentially significant without proving falsification.

The highest-value evidence would be native drafts, dictation, transcription records, timestamps, audit logs and the source of the patient-attributed language.

8. Harm

The later record documents substantial burdens: persistent painful foot symptoms, mobility restriction, loss of activities, work and functional difficulty, distress and deterioration in quality of life.

For legal purposes, however, harm and causation must remain separate.

The fact that harm occurred does not prove that earlier clinical handling caused it. Some harm may arise from the underlying PRKN disease, structural foot pathology, neuropathic mechanisms, treatment effects or other factors.

The legal question is what additional harm, if any, was avoidable under a non-negligent pathway. That requires expert evidence.

9. Causation

The counterfactual should be expressed concretely rather than rhetorically.

If the relationship had been proportionately reassessed after notice in 2017, what probably would have happened?

Possible counterfactual steps might include earlier movement-disorders characterisation; explicit recognition of dystonia or mixed mechanisms; different follow-up ownership; earlier targeted treatment or medication trials; different pain management; clearer return conditions; or reduced delay in integrating later boundary-crossing evidence.

Would those differences probably have reduced pain, functional loss, psychological injury, work loss or other harm, and by how much?

That is a medical and legal expert question. Later treatment response and PRKN biology can inform it, but cannot prove it alone.

10. Prima facie analytical case

For personal understanding, the following is the strongest prima facie analytical pathway. It is not a legal conclusion.

  1. Duty: specialist care and competent record-making were owed.
  2. Material clinical relationship: the pre-summary record contained genuine structural feet, severe Parkinsonism and directly observed dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology.
  3. Notice: before closure, the responsible clinician was directly told the originating problem remained inadequately addressed.
  4. Opportunity: corrective options remained available before authoritative finalisation.
  5. Potential breach: the present record does not demonstrate a comparably explicit post-notice reassessment, preservation of uncertainty or reasoned closure before the separation acquired documentary authority.
  6. Propagation: the resulting information state travelled and shaped later care.
  7. Stress tests: later independent observations repeatedly crossed the original boundary.
  8. Limited historical reconstruction: those observations were predominantly accommodated locally rather than visibly used to reconstruct the founding allocation.
  9. Harm: substantial later pain, functional limitation and distress are documented.
  10. Causation still to prove: expert evidence must establish whether a competent alternative pathway would probably have prevented or reduced that harm.

If any material step fails, the legal case narrows accordingly.

11. The strongest innocent legal explanation

This was a difficult very-young-onset Parkinsonian presentation. Severe Parkinsonism was correctly recognised. Structural foot disease was real. A separate peripheral component was medically plausible. The patient's own history supported some phenomenological distinction. The clinician appropriately considered genetics, sought outside advice and documented a cautious treatment strategy. The Protest may have been understood as a podiatric-treatment concern rather than a challenge to the neurological model. Later treatment response is compatible with mixed mechanisms. A later PRKN/dystonia diagnosis does not prove that the 2017 process fell below the standard of care.

That explanation remains coherent unless disproved by provenance or expert evidence.

12. The strongest concerning legal explanation

The competing hypothesis is that the responsible service knew before closure that a central part of the presenting problem remained unresolved, but did not perform or preserve the proportionate relational inquiry needed to close it. The final synthesis then gave a peripheral/separate explanation sufficient documentary authority to travel, while the patient's contrary notice did not travel with it. Later observations repeatedly pressured the original allocation, but were absorbed through local adaptations. When challenged, the mature longitudinal record—especially the exceptionally well-documented levodopa strategy—was used to demonstrate reasonableness, and governance validated the accumulated pathway without visibly reconstructing the founding state from primary evidence.

This remains a hypothesis until proof and expert evidence discriminate it from the innocent account.

13. Governance as a separate proof problem

An institutional review is not automatically independent merely because it is multidisciplinary or later in time.

The questions are: What were the Terms of Reference? What primary sources were examined? Was the pre-discharge notice treated as a material allegation? Did reviewers distinguish inherited repetition from independent corroboration? Did they reconstruct the pre-summary information state? Did they test how the founding allocation acquired authority? Did they know the accuracy/completeness of that information state was itself disputed?

The visible outcome can be entirely reasonable and still leave these proof questions unanswered.

14. The originating clinician and later defence

The originating clinician occupies a distinct evidential position.

If he personally received a material pre-closure objection that the record omitted, he cannot later be treated as though his knowledge is limited to the record itself.

The later defence therefore raises two different questions:

  1. Was the clinical position sincerely and reasonably held?
  2. Did the later explanation accurately account for material events known personally to the author but absent from the chart?

The first is primarily clinical. The second is about provenance and completeness. Neither should be converted into a dishonesty allegation without evidence.

15. Mens rea and stronger allegations

The current analytical structure supports questions about function and knowledge state. It does not establish deliberate falsification, concealment or conspiracy.

observable documentary effect → what the actor knew/appreciated → whether selection was conscious → whether any omission or alteration was intended to mislead.

Native production history is crucial because it can distinguish ordinary compressed synthesis from knowingly strengthened or deliberately selective synthesis.

16. Proof priorities

The most probative next evidence is not another routine clinic note.

Priority evidence includes dictation/audio for the 2017 synthesis; drafts and version history; timestamps and audit logs; source notes for patient-attributed phrases; any post-Protest examination or discussion; full information sent to the external 2017 specialist; clinical video; full PAR/SIMT evidence pack and Terms of Reference; appropriately instructed independent movement-disorders expert evidence; and expert evidence on causation and avoidable harm.

17. What this case is, in legal-facing form

The mature theory is not a simple delayed-diagnosis claim.

It is a possible information-state negligence case in which the alleged breach sits at the point where an unresolved clinical relationship was transformed into an authoritative travelling account after direct corrective notice.

The later pathway matters because it may reveal the consequences of that phase change: inheritance, repeated local assimilation, defensive use of the mature record, and institutional validation.

The legal question remains conventional in one crucial sense: was the care below the required standard, and did that breach cause compensable harm?

What is unusual is where the alleged breach must be reconstructed: before the record became the thing later reviewers relied upon to understand what happened.
