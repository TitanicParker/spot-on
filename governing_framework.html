A functioning clinical system must preserve a route by which materially contrary evidence can change an existing decision. When an explanation is doing work that permits closure, separation of problems, discharge, or continued reliance on an established model, the explanation cannot legitimately substitute for the evidence that would test it when such testing remains reasonably available, clinically warranted and materially relevant.

An explanation cannot legitimately substitute for the evidence that would test the explanation when testing remains reasonably available, clinically warranted and materially relevant.

This case is governed by that distinction. The July 2017 discharge synthesis is not notable for lack of neurological sophistication. It is extraordinarily sophisticated. The controlling question is what that sophistication was being used to do while a clinically material relationship remained unresolved and, on the Protest premise, had been expressly raised before closure.

The object of analysis is therefore one integrated system: evidence, explanation, decision, patient, record, later clinical interpretation and institutional validation. The original decision and the patient's epistemic status are not separate themes. A decision becomes self-sealing precisely when contrary evidence can be preserved only by changing what the patient, the symptoms, or the later facts are allowed to mean.

Everything in the repository should be read through this governing structure.

0. Repository role, legal alignment and document order

This document is intended to sit at the top of the repository's analytical hierarchy. It does not outrank the evidence. It governs what the repository is permitted to infer from the evidence, how strongly it may state those inferences, and how the legal-facing and narrative documents should relate to one another.

Factual authority

Originals and canonical record

The original source controls over transcription, summary and analysis. The scoped canonical evidential record controls source status, provenance, contemporaneity and evidential independence inside the public repository. If an analytical sentence conflicts with a source, the source wins.

Analytical authority

This governing framework

This document controls the distinction between observation, report, interpretation, hypothesis, later evidence and institutional conclusion; the distinction between reason and rationalisation; and the level of inference that may properly be carried into the legal and ethical documents.

Legal application

The Legal Shape of the Case

The legal-facing document translates the governed analytical propositions into questions of notice, breach, record integrity, professional regulation, institutional governance, causation and proof. It may translate; it may not silently promote a conditional analytical hypothesis into an established fact or legal conclusion.

The controlling relationship to the legal-facing document

The Legal Shape of the Case already identifies the strongest theory as something other than a hindsight allegation that dystonia should simply have been diagnosed. It focuses on an unresolved clinically material relationship, the alleged pre-discharge Protest, the completed specialist synthesis, the separate peripheral/structural future allocated to the feet, the travelling record, later care, governance and proof. This framework now supplies the deeper rule that governs those legal propositions.

A legal argument may defend or criticise a decision by reference to the evidence, reasoning and proportionate inquiry that actually supported it. It may not treat an increasingly sophisticated explanation as though it were itself the missing evidence or clinical act.

Accordingly, the legal-facing document should be read subject to the following rules:

  • Source before theory. Contemporaneous observation, patient report, clinician interpretation, later external evidence and institutional conclusion must remain distinguishable.
  • Notice remains a proof question. The Protest is analytically central if established, but the retained record presently establishes it as later patient evidence unless and until independent contemporaneous corroboration or provenance evidence proves more.
  • Plausibility is not evidential completion. “Peripheral neuropathy,” alcohol causation, structural feet, Parkinsonism, dystonia and mixed mechanisms can all be medically plausible. The legal question is what evidential and clinical work each proposition was permitted to perform.
  • Chronology and conduct distinguish reason from rationalisation. The same words can be legitimate prospective reasoning or retrospective/self-protective explanation. Draft history, timing, clinical acts and provenance are therefore central.
  • Later biology is a stress test, not backwards proof. Later dystonia and PRKN evidence may test the durability of the 2017 model; it cannot be projected backwards as information the 2017 clinicians necessarily possessed.
  • Later notes are dependency-sensitive evidence. Repetition inside an inherited information architecture is not automatically independent confirmation of the founding allocation.
  • The patient and decision must be analysed together. If the original decision remains fixed by making the patient's contrary evidence increasingly interpretable, the patient's epistemic demotion and the decision's epistemic privilege are one mechanism.
  • Negligence remains an expert question. The framework can identify notice, opportunity, documentation–action mismatch and possible self-sealing. Whether reasonable specialist care in 2017 required different action, and what damage flowed from any breach, requires properly instructed expert and legal analysis.
  • Defensive purpose is not presumed. “Anticipatory defensive documentation,” “self-protective reconstruction” and similar descriptions remain hypotheses whose strength depends on provenance, drafts, communications, clinical action and audit evidence. They are not synonyms for falsification.
  • The theory must be able to lose. Evidence that the separation language substantially pre-dated the Protest, that a targeted relational assessment occurred, that the Protest concerned a materially different proposition, or that the final language followed genuinely new clinical information must be capable of weakening or defeating the stronger theory.

1. The controlling question

A functioning clinical system must preserve some route by which contrary facts can change an existing model.

The relevant contrast is between two rules:

When a material concern is raised, investigate it proportionately, then document why the resulting decision is reasonable.

and:

When a material concern is raised, construct a sufficiently plausible account under which the existing decision remains reasonable.

The first rule is compatible with learning. The second is not.

This distinction does not require every conceivable test to be performed. Medicine is full of uncertainty, proportionality and reasonable decisions not to investigate further. The problem arises when explanatory propositions are allowed to acquire the evidential force of inquiry merely because they are plausible.

A sentence such as:

“The burning feet could represent an independent peripheral neuropathy.”

can be an excellent clinical hypothesis if it generates or follows a proportionate process capable of testing, limiting or preserving uncertainty around it.

The same sentence can function as rationalisation if it appears or gains force after the existing disposition has been challenged, is used to explain why that challenge need not alter the disposition, and is not exposed to the reasonably available evidence capable of distinguishing it from alternatives.

The words alone cannot distinguish a reason from a rationalisation.

Chronology and conduct do.

The question for this case is therefore:

Was expertise being used to reduce uncertainty about reality, or to reduce the vulnerability of an existing decision to contrary reality?

That is the spine connecting the 2017 discharge note, the later clinic notes, the complaint response, the governance process, and the changing epistemic status of the patient.

2. Evidential discipline: what this note may and may not claim

The public repository contains a scoped canonical evidential record, not the complete master corpus. The canonical selection retains 32 documentary objects from a larger 83-document corpus. The complete corpus is said to exist elsewhere but is not all available in the public repository.

Accordingly, negative propositions must remain documentary negatives unless independently proved.

The analysis should distinguish four categories:

  1. Directly demonstrated in the available contemporaneous record.
  2. Later evidence capable of testing the earlier model, but not of becoming knowledge available in 2017.
  3. Analytical inference about the function or architecture of the record.
  4. Allegation or assumed premise requiring separate proof.

Several rules follow.

  • “Not visible in the retained record” is not equivalent to “never happened.”
  • The later PRKN result cannot be projected backwards as proof of what clinicians should have known in July 2017.
  • The later patient account of the Protest is evidence that the allegation was made; it is not the same thing as contemporaneous corroboration of the event.
  • “Burning first becomes visible in the disclosed record within the later synthesis” is supportable; “burning was invented” is not.
  • The peripheral-neuropathy possibility was tentative, not a proven diagnosis.
  • Immediate levodopa is not an appropriate hindsight “missing obvious test” theory; the actual 2017 record documents a deliberate strategy of caution in a very young patient.
  • Podiatry referral was real and appropriate. A destination, however, is not necessarily an explanation of the relationship.
  • A public summary assertion that Podiatry recorded "No neuropathy" should not presently carry primary evidential weight: in the retained canonical referral form the visible "No neuropathy" box is described as not ticked. A later source may exist in the wider corpus, but it should be located before the proposition is relied upon.

The inquiry must not become self-sealing in the opposite direction. The hypothesis developed here must itself remain falsifiable by provenance, expert evidence and contrary source material.

3. The 2017 factual substrate: the case did not arrive pre-separated

The incoming problem was integrated.

Before specialist admission, the record joins:

  • bilateral foot pain;
  • abnormal gait;
  • marked mallet toes, calluses and pressure areas;
  • poor foot condition;
  • “muscle tension”;
  • increased tone in both legs;
  • brisk reflexes;
  • toe clawing;
  • worsening dexterity;
  • initiation difficulty;
  • deteriorating work function;
  • and possible Parkinsonism or another neurological disorder.

The June urgent referral therefore did not present Neurology with a cleanly separated case called “Parkinsonism plus unrelated feet.” It presented an integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem.

Podiatry was already involved. On 6 July the GP recorded that Podiatry would mark the case as a priority “as Parkinson's.” That matters because it shows that podiatric management and neurological relevance were not inherently mutually exclusive. Structural care could coexist with neurological ownership of the relationship.

The specialist history also contained genuine evidence favouring distinction. The patient had longstanding corns, calluses and fallen arches, had received foot treatment since about 2009–10, and reported that after leaving bar work the foot pain reduced substantially, to a degree “out of the picture,” while leg tension continued and corns/calluses remained. He therefore came to recognise that foot pain and leg tension were “not the same problem.”

That is important evidence. Separation was not fabricated from nothing.

But the correct inference is narrower:

Phenomenological distinction had evidence. Biological independence remained an inference.

At the same time, Neurology directly observed the competing neurological relationship. The gait was described as “strikingly abnormal.” The apparent footdrop was “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.” The left lower limb “kicks out like a foot drop.” Severe rigidity and bradykinesia were present. Pes planus and hammertoes were documented in the same examination.

So by the founding admission the service held, in the same information state:

real structural feet + real pain history + real phenomenological distinction + real severe Parkinsonism + real dystonic-looking lower-limb movement.

The unresolved object was visible:

What is the relationship between the painful / structurally abnormal feet and the dystonic-looking lower limb?

4. Sophistication was present, and that makes the problem harder

The admission was not intellectually thin.

The discharge synthesis considered:

  • very-young-onset Parkinsonism;
  • atypical phenotype;
  • recessive genetics;
  • PRKN/PARK2;
  • PARK6;
  • PARK7;
  • PARK9, expressly linked to “the neuropathy question”;
  • other Parkinson-plus and metabolic possibilities;
  • imaging;
  • prognosis;
  • treatment sequence;
  • levodopa sensitivity and dyskinesia risk;
  • future whole-exome or whole-genome analysis;
  • and the limitations of the author's own expertise, including the express statement: “I am not a movement disorders neurologist.”

There was a Neurology Complex Case meeting. Senior colleagues were involved. External genetic advice was sought. The note was intended to consolidate discussions, assist “debriefing” and set out a clear plan.

Therefore the mature question is not:

“Why was sophisticated neurological thought unavailable?”

It is:

Why was extraordinary neurological sophistication available for disease subtype, genetics, prognosis and treatment, yet the concrete relationship between painful structural feet and a directly observed dystonic-looking limb did not receive equivalent relational development?

Even that question is still incomplete, because it treats sophistication as neutral background.

The harder possibility is that the sophisticated synthesis was itself the means by which the unresolved relationship acquired a separate future.

The discharge note must therefore be read not merely as a description of thought but as an act of organisation.

5. The discharge synthesis as a constitutive act

Before synthesis, the record contains observations that can still be organised in several ways.

The final synthesis gives those observations an architecture.

One branch becomes neurologically elaborate:

Parkinsonism → very young onset → atypical phenotype → recessive inheritance → PRKN/PARK2 → PARK6/PARK7/PARK9 → imaging → prognosis → treatment strategy.

The other becomes:

corns/calluses + burning + foot pain → “leg symptoms” → may be completely separate → perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy → perhaps alcohol-related → not clear / improved → Podiatry.

The important analytical point is not that every element in the second branch is false. Several are medically plausible. The important point is what the branch does.

It gives the foot territory an explanatory and managerial route that no longer requires the feet to interrogate the Parkinsonian/dystonic model.

The unresolved bridge:

painful/structural feet ↔︎ dystonic-looking lower limb

receives much less development than either branch on its own.

This is the physically created frame.

The note does not merely contain facts. It assigns facts to categories, categories to causal possibilities, and causal possibilities to future owners.

That is why the discharge summary is the founding epistemic instrument of the later case.

It determines not only what later readers will know, but what kinds of questions later facts will be permitted to ask.

6. The introduced material: what each phrase does inside the frame

Several phrases should be read together because their force lies in sequence and function.

“Out of the picture”

The historical fact is that foot pain reduced after leaving bar work, while leg tension continued and corns/calluses remained.

That supports phenomenological difference.

Inside the later architecture, however, the phrase can also reduce the contemporary evidential weight of the foot problem. A historical reduction in pain can begin to function as support for separation.

“Burning”

“Burning” first becomes visible in the disclosed record in the compact neuropathy synthesis. It may well have been genuinely reported; the scoped record cannot establish otherwise.

Its function is nevertheless important:

burning → neuropathic quality → peripheral neuropathy → possible alcohol-related cause.

A sensory descriptor helps open a respectable peripheral explanatory route.

“Leg symptoms including corns and callouses and burning”

This grouping matters. Corns and calluses are structural/cutaneous findings. Burning is a sensory descriptor. “Leg symptoms” is a broader anatomical category.

The synthesis turns these heterogeneous observations into a single object capable of travelling into a peripheral explanation.

“May have been completely separate” / “perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy” / alcohol / “not clear as this has improved”

The language is tentative. That must be preserved.

But tentativeness does not mean lack of organising force. A tentative hypothesis can still structure ownership, follow-up and future interpretation.

The question is whether the hypothesis remained visibly provisional in the sense of having an exit condition, or became operationally sufficient for separation without the relationship being tested.

“He has in his own mind — markedly divorced…”

This may be one of the most consequential sentences in the document.

The patient is used as evidence supporting the separation of tension from corns, calluses and foot pain.

If the Protest is proved, the same patient also supplied contrary evidence: the unresolved bilateral foot problem remained important enough that he objected to closure.

The potential asymmetry is therefore not merely omission of a complaint.

It is:

the patient's confirmatory distinctions are preserved as evidence for the frame, while the patient's disconfirmatory challenge to the frame does not acquire equivalent travelling force.

That is a change in the epistemic status of the patient.

“PARK9 (the neuropathy question)”

This phrase matters because neuropathy is not merely an incidental non-neurological aside. It enters the high-level neurological/genetic architecture itself.

The note is sophisticated enough to conceptualise neuropathy as part of a monogenic Parkinsonism question, while the concrete feet–dystonia relationship remains comparatively underdeveloped.

“I am not a movement disorders neurologist”

This statement is candid and can be read innocently as appropriate recognition of limits.

But once a very young patient has severe Parkinsonism, dystonic-looking lower-limb movement, structural foot pathology and an unresolved relational question, acknowledgement of a specialist limitation also raises a specific epistemic issue:

Did recognition of the limit generate a proportionate route for the unresolved relationship to encounter the expertise capable of testing it?

The point is not that referral was automatically required. It is that disclosure of limitation without a corresponding exit route can become another part of a document that explains uncertainty without necessarily resolving or preserving it.

7. The Protest: from unresolved problem to pre-closure error signal

The Protest is the largest evidential hinge and must remain conditionally framed.

The retained canonical record does not contemporaneously corroborate the bedside event. The later patient account alleges that during the 10–11 July 2017 teaching encounter, while still an inpatient, he said that the disabling bilateral foot problem had not been addressed, asked for the supervising neurologist, and used the “full NCT / bald tires” analogy.

If the Protest is not proved, the case still contains:

  • an integrated incoming problem;
  • structural foot disease;
  • directly observed dystonic-looking lower-limb movement;
  • a plausible peripheral branch;
  • and a travelling synthesis that does not visibly develop their relationship.

That supports an incomplete relational investigation / documentary asymmetry thesis.

If the Protest is proved, the character of the case changes materially.

The patient did not merely discover years later that the account was incomplete. He identified the incompleteness before closure, while the care episode and authoritative synthesis were still open. The documentary openness is independently visible: the 13 July 2017 DaTscan, performed after physical discharge, was incorporated into the still-open discharge product by addendum before finalisation. That does not prove further bedside examination remained possible after discharge; it does prove that the authoritative information state remained capable of augmentation.

The Protest then functions as a pre-closure error signal:

You have not accounted for this central problem. If you complete the episode as though you have, the incompleteness itself will become part of the care pathway.

That is what gives meaning to the patient's phrase “prior negligence setup.”

It should not be treated as a legal conclusion. It identifies a hypothesis about timing and function:

Once a potentially negligent incompleteness had been expressly identified before closure, did the system use its remaining opportunity principally to investigate and preserve that unresolved question, or principally to construct an account under which the existing disposition remained defensible?

This is earlier than ordinary post-event defensiveness. On the hypothesis, the future complaint exists in substance before the patient has even left the episode of care.

The clinical problem is:

What is the relationship between these feet and this abnormal movement?

The institutional problem, once the inadequacy of the disposition is challenged, may become:

Can the intended disposition remain reasonable despite the challenge?

Those two problems can recruit the same neurological sophistication for different purposes.

That is the central meaning of the possible negative employment of expertise.

8. The choice-space was broad: prevention did not require solving the whole case

The Protest, if proved, did not create a binary between restarting the entire admission and ignoring the concern.

The middle was large.

Possible proportionate responses included:

  • focused bedside re-examination;
  • history reconstruction from the feet outward;
  • explicit phenomenological mapping of pain, burning, pressure lesions, deformity, toe clawing, rigidity, dystonic posture and subjective tension;
  • a mixed-mechanism formulation in which structural pathology and neurological loading/posture could coexist;
  • movement-disorders or colleague review focused specifically on the lower-limb/foot phenomenology;
  • a defined neurological question for Podiatry;
  • explicit preservation of “independent vs interacting remains unresolved”;
  • documentation of the patient's disagreement;
  • a defined neurological return loop after imaging or treatment;
  • or proceeding with discharge while recording why no further inpatient inquiry was proportionate and what finding would trigger reconsideration.

The record already shows general consultation and general follow-up. Therefore the criticism cannot fairly be “there was no consultation” or “there was no return.”

The narrower question is:

Was there a specifically owned return loop for the unresolved relationship?

Likewise, the missing opportunity should not be reduced to immediate levodopa. The actual record documents legitimate caution about early dopaminergic treatment in a very young patient, and the local 2017 academic literature supports the use of anticholinergic treatment such as procyclidine in comparable recessive Parkinsonism scenarios.

The relevant missing middle is therefore about inquiry, integration, ownership, preservation of uncertainty and exit conditions.

The proportionality formulation is:

The issue is not whether Neurology had to solve the feet before discharge. It is whether the authority and durability of the disposition became greater than the relational work visibly supporting it.

9. Documentation–action mismatch and epistemic self-sealing

The central institutional danger is a documentation–action mismatch.

Compare:

material anomaly → proportionate inquiry → decision → documentation

with:

material anomaly → existing decision → challenge → increasingly sophisticated explanation → unchanged decision.

In the second sequence, explanation may begin to perform work that investigation would ordinarily perform.

That is where a model can become epistemically self-sealing.

A self-sealing model does not need to deny contrary facts. It can absorb them.

  • Structural pain? Separate foot disease.
  • Burning? Neuropathy.
  • Alcohol history? Possible etiology.
  • Footdrop appearance with normal power? Parkinsonian/dystonic phenomenon.
  • Later OFF cramping? OFF dystonia.
  • Pain and shuffling responding to dopaminergic timing? OFF-related symptom amplification.
  • Profound relief with Sinemet CR? Levodopa may reduce pain experience whatever its cause.
  • Later dystonia diagnosis? Update present care, but prior governance need not be reopened.

Each individual proposition may be plausible.

The problem is the role they collectively play if no observation is allowed to make the founding partition lose.

The deepest falsifiability question is therefore:

What fact, had it occurred, would have required the original feet–movement relationship to be reconstructed?

If every possible fact can be incorporated without changing the epistemic status of the founding decision, the model has ceased to function as a genuinely testable clinical model.

10. The patient and the decision are one epistemic mechanism

The epistemic privilege of the original decision and the epistemic downgrading of the patient are not two separate consequences.

They are the same operation.

The system preserves the epistemic status of the original decision by reducing the epistemic force of the patient as a source of disconfirming knowledge.

Once the founding frame exists, contradictory information has to move somewhere. If the decision is not permitted to move, the contradictory element must become flexible instead.

In this case, the most flexible element is the patient.

His symptoms can be reclassified.

His chronology can be redescribed.

His phenomenological distinctions can be recruited when they support separation.

His objection can be omitted, narrowed, mediated or treated as a disagreement about interpretation.

His later treatment response can be explained without reconstructing the original relationship.

His later diagnosis can change the present without changing the historical institutional conclusion.

The structure is therefore:

decision fixed; patient made interpretable.

A patient does not need to be medically correct to have epistemic standing. The patient possesses evidence clinicians do not possess: chronology, pain, functional change, previous baseline, treatment-state experience, and the meaning of his own distinctions.

In a functioning clinical system, that evidence should have two roles:

  1. populate the clinical history;
  2. retain some capacity to challenge the clinician's interpretation of that history.

In a self-sealing system, the first role survives while the second weakens.

The patient becomes:

credible when confirmatory; corrigible when contradictory.

That is why selective preservation is not merely a record-keeping issue. It is one of the mechanisms by which a decision can become resistant to correction.

The strongest paired formulations are:

The original decision becomes authoritative to the extent that the patient becomes interpretable.

and:

The system could change almost everything except the epistemic status of the original decision because it could reinterpret almost everything about the patient instead.

This is not a second problem alongside incorrigibility. It is the mechanism of incorrigibility.

11. The later clinic notes are not independent confirmations; they are stress tests of the inherited model

Once the 2017 synthesis travels, later clinicians do not encounter the raw 2017 presentation. They encounter:

patient + accumulated record + inherited categories.

Therefore repetition in later notes cannot automatically be counted as independent corroboration of the founding partition.

The later record should instead be read as a sequence of stress tests:

What happens when new reality presses against the original architecture?

2018: treatment-state evidence touches the foot territory

When procyclidine was withdrawn for about two weeks, one of the first rapidly changing problems was the return of the outside-left-foot corn problem; the patient also became more “shifty” and “unrelaxed,” and procyclidine was restarted.

This does not prove the corn was caused by dystonia. It does create dynamic evidence in the disputed territory: changing movement-disorder treatment changed what happened in or around the foot problem.

The founding relationship was not visibly reconstructed.

2019: mechanism differentiation is demonstrably possible

The neurologist described “dystonia type elements” and mild akathesic restlessness and, in another problem, carefully distinguished a shoulder/triceps syndrome as probably not primarily Parkinsonian while acknowledging that Parkinsonism could magnify pain.

This is relevant because it shows the clinician could distinguish mechanisms and mixed causation when he chose to do so. It does not itself answer the foot relationship.

2020: apparent footdrop is explicitly reclassified as neurological

The clinician reassessed a left lower-limb “throw” that looked like footdrop and concluded it was “actually parkinsonian” because dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion and eversion power were normal.

This is an important local correction. A phenomenon in precisely the disputed territory is reclassified neurologically.

But the correction behaves like a patch, not a reconstruction of the founding model. The note does not visibly ask what this means for the earlier separation between neurological movement and painful/structural feet.

2022: foot pain remains in the longitudinal record

The feet took 20–25 minutes to “warm up” in the mornings; they were sore; corns/calluses and a “coronal” pain were described; the patient wondered whether the pain was neuralgic.

Again the foot territory remains active, but the founding relationship is not reconstructed.

2023: the closest apparent reopening becomes assimilation

The patient “ground to an absolute halt with foot pain in both feet,” had severe walking difficulty and cramping in the left lower limb.

The clinician explicitly asked whether the cramping was Parkinsonian dystonia and said that, if dystonic, it was “clearly more likely an OFF dystonia.” The clinician also asked whether much of the inability to walk was due to insufficient levodopa, and the patient reported Stalevo gave “pep in step” and helped “for pain.” The Parkinson's nurse later assessed leg pain and shuffling as OFF related.

Most importantly, the clinician recognised a misalignment: he might appear to think everything was explainable by Parkinson's, while the patient had reasonable concerns that much related to the feet as a separate problem.

This is almost the founding relational question returning explicitly.

Yet the model is not visibly reconstructed. Instead, the new evidence is accommodated:

  • OFF dystonia can exist;
  • OFF walking impairment can exist;
  • Parkinsonism can magnify pain;
  • a separate foot problem can still exist;
  • Fabry disease can be tested as a Parkinsonism/peripheral-neuropathy overlap possibility.

This is better described as:

assimilation of counter-evidence without correction of the founding partition.

2024: further accommodation

Pregabalin substantially improves foot pain; toe curling remains; Fabry and beta-hexosaminidase testing are normal; concern about alcohol-related neuropathy remains; thiamine is recommended; dopaminergic treatment is balanced against dyskinesia.

Again the clinical system adapts locally.

March 2025: potentially falsifying treatment response is absorbed

The feet are now a central concern: the first 150 steps are described as “torture,” and the patient says relief comes when Parkinson medication hits the system.

The clinician allows that Sinemet commonly makes pain feel better but argues that pain is not necessarily a direct Parkinsonian phenomenon; painful dystonia exists, but the described pain may still have a separate cause. Sinemet CR is added and the later complaint response describes profound relief.

That profound dopaminergic response could reopen the original relationship. It could support dystonia, non-motor OFF pain, mixed mechanisms, or another Parkinson-related explanation. It does not prove any one of them.

What matters is that the founding architecture can absorb even this:

Sinemet may reduce the experience of pain whatever its underlying cause.

That proposition may be medically reasonable. Functionally, however, it permits dramatic response in the disputed foot territory to coexist with continued separation.

By this point the critical question is no longer whether the model has been locally updated. It clearly has.

The question is:

What evidence is actually capable of forcing the original partition to be reconsidered?

12. Local adaptability is not global corrigibility

The longitudinal record demonstrates substantial local adaptability:

  • medication changes;
  • treatment trials;
  • local phenomenological reclassification;
  • recognition of OFF dystonia;
  • recognition of OFF-related shuffling/pain;
  • new metabolic/genetic tests;
  • acceptance of profound Sinemet response;
  • and later external diagnostic/genetic clarification.

But the relevant unit of analysis is not whether anything could change.

It is whether the founding feet–movement partition could lose its epistemic status.

Through February 2026, the retained record shows no clear internal Galway reconstruction of that founding relationship.

The better vocabulary is therefore:

local adaptability under global incorrigibility;

assimilation without correction;

clinical plasticity without epistemic correction.

The concise formulation is:

The system could change almost everything except the epistemic status of the original decision.

And because the patient and decision are one mechanism:

It could do so because almost everything about the patient remained available for reinterpretation.

13. The 2025 complaint response is second-order defence of the architecture

By March 2025, the issue is no longer merely clinical management. The patient explicitly alleges anchoring, alcohol-related neuropathy framing and failure to reassess, and points to the marked benefit from Sinemet CR.

The clinician's response is important because it changes the level of analysis.

The question is now:

Was my previous practice reasonable and open to correction?

The response points to:

  • alternative investigations;
  • Fabry testing;
  • repeated DaTscan;
  • medication evolution;
  • the fact that Sinemet had been introduced since 2021;
  • the use of pregabalin to limit dopaminergic escalation;
  • acknowledgement in 2017 that levodopa might later be needed;
  • and the recent profound relief from Sinemet CR.

These facts demonstrate that care changed.

But there is a category problem if those local changes are used as the answer to a different question:

Was the founding feet–movement relationship itself ever reconstructed?

“I changed many things” is not logically equivalent to “I revisited the specific causal partition under challenge.”

The complaint response can therefore function as second-order validation: the adaptability of later management is used to defend the epistemic openness of the originating model.

That is precisely where local plasticity can be mistaken for global corrigibility.

The response need not be dishonest to perform this function.

It becomes explicitly advocacy-capable because it is now organising the longitudinal record in defence of the reasonableness of the earlier process.

14. Governance is the next recursive layer

The governance process should not be read as wholly independent of the clinical record it reviews.

Initially, QPS classifies the complaint as matters solely of clinical judgment and routes it to the original neurologist. After dissatisfaction, it is escalated through the Incident Management Framework to a Preliminary Assessment Report and Serious Incident Management Team.

The September 2025 SIMT concludes that there was “no deficit in the care provided.”

The retained source does not show the full evidence pack or a source-by-source reconstruction of the original feet–movement relationship.

The epistemic issue is therefore not simply that governance disagreed with the patient.

It is the dependency structure:

2017 decision

→ 2017 explanatory record

→ later care interpreted through inherited architecture

→ later notes generated inside that architecture

→ original clinician uses later notes to demonstrate continuing reassessment

→ governance reviews accumulated record and response

→ governance validates the care

→ validation becomes additional institutional support for the original decision.

This is recursive validation.

Each layer appears more authoritative than the one before it while potentially remaining epistemically dependent upon the founding allocation.

That does not make the governance outcome wrong by definition. It means that governance can only count as a genuine corrective mechanism if it is capable of reconstructing the provenance and testing the founding frame rather than merely reviewing the coherence of the record produced under it.

The decisive governance question is:

Did the review independently test the disputed relationship and the information state at the moment of closure, or did it principally assess whether the accumulated record contained a plausible account under which the care remained defensible?

15. Data governance and historical stabilisation

The 2025 Article 16 rectification process introduces another layer.

The clinical-data refusal says, in substance, that the diagnosis/opinion accurately reflects the professional opinion held at the time and that retaining the record in its current form serves continuity of care.

That principle can be entirely legitimate. Historical records should not simply be rewritten because later evidence or disagreement arises.

But the function of the record changes.

Originally the question is:

Is this clinical account true enough and sufficiently tested to guide care?

Later it becomes:

Was this account a reasonable professional opinion?

Later still:

Does this record accurately preserve that this was the opinion held at the time?

Those are different truth conditions.

A proposition can be medically wrong yet still accurately record a historical opinion.

So the record can move through three statuses:

clinical hypothesis → travelling specialist account → protected historical record.

That process can be appropriate as data governance and still have an epistemic consequence: the original frame becomes progressively harder to interrogate because the institutional question has shifted from whether it was true enough to whether it faithfully records that it was once believed.

16. January–February 2026: the endpoint of the architecture

The January 2026 QPS response is particularly important because it makes the distinction explicit.

QPS states that the complaint assessment and SIMT process were appropriate based on the contemporaneous data, and that a subsequent dystonia diagnosis in December 2025 does not undermine those processes or outcomes. It further states that the updated diagnosis does not necessitate reopening governance reviews, altering prior determinations, or retrospectively amending historical medical records, and that QPS identifies no current governance risk arising from continued reliance on the completed processes.

This is not merely persistence of a clinical opinion.

It is an institutional rule about the epistemic status of the historical decision:

the present may change without requiring the past determination to become newly contestable.

In February 2026 an external consultant then records that genetic testing shows two pathogenic Parkin variants and concludes that the patient's Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound-heterozygous PRKN mutations.

This is an external biological clarification. It does not prove every foot symptom was dystonic, that neuropathy never existed, or that 2017 clinicians should have known the genotype.

Its legitimate retrospective force is narrower:

the dystonic branch that was already clinically visible in 2017 later proved biologically central to the movement disorder.

Yet the correction occurs externally. The retained Galway record through February 2026 does not show a corresponding internal reconstruction of the founding feet–movement relationship after that clarification.

So the final state is striking:

  • the clinical description can change;
  • the genetic explanation can change;
  • treatment can change;
  • movement phenomenology can change;
  • the patient's present diagnosis can change;
  • but the epistemic status of the original institutional decision is expressly protected from those changes.

That is why “local adaptability under global incorrigibility” is not rhetorical exaggeration at the level of the founding model. It describes the architecture visible by the end of the chronology.

17. The record as evidential instrument versus advocacy instrument

A medical record performs more than one function. It supports care, communication, audit, continuity, governance and, inevitably, later legal evaluation.

The problem is not that a good record makes good care easier to defend. It should.

The distinction is:

defensible because the care was appropriately investigated

versus

defensible because the documentation supplies a sufficiently coherent explanation despite unresolved investigation.

If retrospective or post-notice explanatory skill can erase the difference between:

  • “we considered this and tested it”;
  • “we consciously decided testing was unnecessary and recorded why”;
  • “we overlooked it”;
  • “the patient accepted the distinction”;
  • and “the patient expressly disputed the distinction,”

then the record loses part of its value as an evidential instrument.

It becomes increasingly capable of functioning as an advocacy instrument.

That does not require falsification.

Distortion can occur through selection, arrangement, omission and unequal preservation while every surviving sentence remains literally defensible.

The danger is particularly acute where the same clinician or service is simultaneously:

decision-maker + recorder + principal witness + later defender of the decision.

That combination is unavoidable to some degree in medicine. It is why contemporaneous recording, independent observations, audit trails, preservation of contrary information and governance independence matter.

They prevent explanatory authority from becoming equivalent to the underlying event.

18. Expertise can increase corrigibility or increase rationalising power

Expertise should increase the ability to discriminate between competing explanations.

But expertise also increases the number and sophistication of explanations available.

Without a mechanism forcing those explanations to encounter disconfirming evidence, more expertise can paradoxically create a more resilient self-sealing model.

The institution then risks selecting for the wrong capacity:

the ability to make difficult situations sound reasonable rather than the ability to discover whether they are reasonable.

That is why the discharge note's sophistication is not automatically reassuring and not automatically incriminating.

Its evidential meaning depends on function.

Was PRKN/PARK2, PARK6, PARK7, PARK9, neuropathy, structural disease and treatment reasoning being used to open discriminating questions?

Or, after the disposition was challenged, did the same expertise provide enough plausible propositions that no contrary fact needed to change the disposition?

The central question is not whether the reasoning was intelligent.

It is whether intelligence remained answerable to evidence capable of defeating it.

19. The local academic capability evidence: relevant, but bounded

The professional environment had conceptual tools directly relevant to the phenomenon.

Timothy Counihan was a co-author of the 2013 JAMA Neurology paper Parkin Disease: A Clinicopathologic Entity?, which includes Parkin cases with prominent foot dystonia.

In August 2017, authors from University Hospital Galway / NUI Galway published Clinical Reasoning: A demure teenager and her dystonic foot, involving apparent footdrop caused by dystonic plantar flexion, normal power/sensation, later compound-heterozygous Parkin mutations and procyclidine treatment.

This evidence shows that the reasoning move:

apparent footdrop → examine power/sensation → recognise dystonia → integrate with recessive Parkinsonism

was not alien to the local academic environment.

But it must not be overused.

The August paper was published after the July admission. The record does not establish that its authors examined this patient, that the treating clinician saw the manuscript before publication, or that the local capability was deployed at this bedside.

Its fair use is therefore:

the conceptual tools existed in the immediate professional environment; the retained record does not establish their specific deployment to this patient's feet–dystonia relationship.

20. What the later biology can and cannot prove

The later PRKN evidence is a retrospective stress test, not a time machine.

It can support:

  • that the dystonic component was biologically central to the movement disorder;
  • that lower-limb dystonia is compatible with PRKN disease;
  • and that the neurological branch observed in 2017 was not an incidental curiosity.

It cannot prove:

  • every corn or callus was caused by dystonia;
  • every episode of foot pain was dystonic;
  • no peripheral neuropathy ever existed;
  • a mixed mechanism was impossible;
  • or clinicians in July 2017 should have known the genotype.

The correct retrospective question is:

Why did the observed neurological branch that later proved biologically central receive less relational development in 2017 than a peripheral branch that remained conditional?

That is a question about explanatory allocation, not hindsight diagnosis.

21. Strongest innocent explanation and strongest concerning explanation

The integrated model must preserve both.

Strongest innocent explanation

This was an unusually difficult very-young-onset Parkinsonian presentation. The treating neurologist genuinely believed the movement disorder and the painful structural/cutaneous foot complaints might be partly independent. The patient's own history supported distinction. Burning reasonably raised neuropathy. Previous alcohol exposure reasonably entered the differential. Podiatry was already involved. The complex discharge note was the ordinary culmination of a long specialist admission. The pending DaTscan justified keeping the document open. No additional inpatient foot investigation was clinically required. The alleged Protest may not have occurred as later remembered, may not have reached the author, may have concerned a different proposition, or may have been regarded as adequately addressed by the substantive clinical account. Later treatment changes demonstrate genuine ongoing care rather than a closed mind. The later dystonia/PRKN clarification does not automatically invalidate the reasonableness of decisions made with 2017 knowledge.

That explanation is coherent and must remain genuinely available.

Strongest concerning explanation

The service knew before closure that the patient regarded the bilateral foot disorder as an unresolved part of the neurological problem and specifically objected to discharge on that basis. Rather than using the remaining opportunity to examine, preserve or specifically route the relationship, the still-open specialist synthesis developed an architecture that de-coupled the foot territory from the movement disorder: historical pain reduction became “out of the picture”; heterogeneous foot and sensory findings were grouped as “leg symptoms”; burning supported neuropathy; previous alcohol supplied an etiology; improvement reduced urgency; selected patient statements supported separation; and Podiatry supplied a destination. The patient's contrary Protest did not travel. Later contradictory observations were repeatedly absorbed through local reclassification and treatment adaptation without reconstructing the founding partition. When the patient later challenged the process, the original clinician used that longitudinal adaptability as evidence against anchoring; governance then relied on the accumulated record and response to validate the care; data governance protected the historical opinion; and QPS ultimately stated that a later dystonia diagnosis did not require reopening the earlier determination.

On that hypothesis, the expertise is genuine but partly functionally adversarial: it helps preserve the decision against the disconfirming force of the patient and later reality.

That is the strongest version of the “prior negligence setup” hypothesis.

It is still a hypothesis.

22. What would discriminate between them

The most important evidence is provenance.

The highest-value questions are:

The Protest

  • Did it occur?
  • What exactly was said?
  • Who heard it?
  • Did the responsible consultant or author of the synthesis know about it before finalisation?
  • Was it documented anywhere contemporaneously?

Drafting chronology

  • When was the discharge synthesis first created?
  • When did the phrases “out of the picture,” “burning,” “completely separate,” “secondary peripheral neuropathy,” alcohol-related, “not clear as this has improved,” and “markedly divorced” first appear?
  • Which passages existed before the alleged Protest?
  • Which were added or strengthened afterwards?
  • What changed after physical discharge?
  • What changed after the DaTscan?

Source and audit evidence

  • dictation audio;
  • transcription records;
  • native drafts;
  • save/edit/sign/finalise timestamps;
  • audit logs;
  • copy-forward history;
  • amendment status;
  • source note for patient-attributed propositions;
  • ward-round, nursing, physiotherapy and occupational-therapy notes;
  • student or teaching records;
  • contemporaneous messages or family communications.

Clinical action after notice

  • repeat foot/lower-limb examination;
  • focused sensory/reflex/strength assessment;
  • movement-phenomenology review;
  • colleague or movement-disorders discussion specifically about the relationship;
  • EMG/NCS consideration where clinically relevant;
  • defined question to Podiatry;
  • mixed-mechanism formulation;
  • explicit reason why no further inpatient investigation was necessary;
  • specific return condition.

Defensive-purpose evidence

The inference becomes materially stronger if contemporaneous communications show that complaint exposure, risk, legal defensibility or concern about the patient's objection influenced the development of the clinical narrative before finalisation.

Risk/governance/legal contact would not itself prove wrongdoing. The contents and timing would matter.

2017 expert standard-of-care evidence

An appropriately instructed neurological expert must answer what a reasonably competent clinician in 2017 should have done once the particular lower-limb/foot findings and, if proved, the patient's objection were known.

Without that step, unresolved diagnosis must not simply be converted into negligence.

The core discriminator remains:

Was the explanation defensible because it followed proportionate inquiry, or did explanation itself substitute for inquiry that was reasonably indicated?

23. The hypothesis must remain falsifiable

The analysis is demanding of the institution because it is demanding about epistemic correction.

It must therefore impose the same demand on itself.

Evidence that would materially weaken the stronger hypothesis includes:

  • proof that the peripheral/separation formulation was substantially complete before the Protest;
  • proof that the Protest never reached the relevant decision-maker;
  • contemporaneous focused examination or consultation demonstrating that the relationship was considered and reasonably left unresolved or separated;
  • a source predating the Protest for the patient-attributed separation language;
  • evidence that post-discharge changes were limited to routine completion and DaTscan incorporation;
  • expert evidence that no additional relational investigation or specialist input was reasonably indicated;
  • or evidence that the patient statements preserved and the later Protest addressed genuinely different propositions rather than contradictory positions on the same relationship.

Evidence that would materially strengthen it includes:

  • corroboration of the Protest before finalisation;
  • post-Protest creation or strengthening of separation/neuropathy/alcohol language without new foot-related clinical evidence;
  • unsupported patient-attributed propositions appearing only after notice;
  • selective omission of contrary contemporaneous information;
  • no targeted clinical act despite remaining opportunity;
  • or contemporaneous communications showing that defensibility rather than additional inquiry had become the relevant concern.

The analysis must be capable of losing.

Otherwise it would reproduce the very epistemic defect it identifies.

The case can now be represented as one continuous epistemic process rather than a series of unrelated problems.

This is why the discharge synthesis, the patient, the later clinic notes, the complaint response and the governance response are all one thing.

They are successive layers in the life of an explanatory decision.

The decisive question at every layer is the same:

What is allowed to change what?

25. Integrated controlling formulation

The strongest current formulation is:

The 2017 problem is not adequately described as a failure to diagnose dystonia, a failure to recognise Parkinsonism, or a simple failure to investigate foot pain. The contemporaneous record shows sophisticated recognition of very-young-onset Parkinsonism, direct observation of dystonic-looking lower-limb movement, genuine structural foot disease, and genuine evidence that the patient experienced some pain and tension as different phenomena. The central question is how those truths were organised. The discharge synthesis appears to give the Parkinsonian syndrome an expansive neurological/genetic future while giving the foot territory a separate/peripheral future through a sequence involving historical improvement, “burning,” possible neuropathy, possible alcohol association, the patient's own supposed separation, and Podiatry. If the Protest is proved, the significance rises sharply: the patient identified the incompleteness before closure, while correction and documentary preservation remained possible. The question then becomes whether expertise was used principally to expose the existing disposition to disconfirming evidence or to make the existing disposition capable of surviving the challenge.

And longitudinally:

The later record does not show a system incapable of change. It shows a system highly capable of changing local facts, treatments and labels while the epistemic status of the founding decision remains unusually durable. That durability and the changing epistemic status of the patient are the same mechanism: the decision can remain fixed because the patient's symptoms, chronology, distinctions, treatment responses and later diagnoses remain available for reinterpretation. Later clinic notes become stress tests of the founding architecture; the complaint response becomes second-order defence of its reasonableness; governance becomes recursive validation of the accumulated record; data governance converts the opinion into protected history; and the January 2026 no-reopen position makes explicit that new clinical reality need not alter the institutional status of the old decision.

The deepest formulation is therefore:

The system could change almost everything except the epistemic status of the original decision because it could reinterpret almost everything about the patient instead.

And the governing prohibition is:

A system may defend a decision by showing the evidence, reasoning and proportionate inquiry that justified it. It cannot make an inadequately investigated decision equivalent to an adequately investigated one merely by increasing the sophistication of the explanation that protects it.

26. Questions that now matter most

The next pass should not broaden the theory further. It should discriminate it.

  1. What exactly was the information state immediately before the alleged Protest?
  2. What exactly was the information state immediately after it?
  3. Which consequential phrases in the discharge synthesis pre-date the Protest, and which post-date it?
  4. What clinical acts, not merely explanatory sentences, occurred after notice?
  5. What would the treating team in 2017 have regarded as evidence against the separate/peripheral allocation?
  6. Was there an explicit exit condition for neuropathy/separation?
  7. Was the patient allowed to transmit disagreement into the authoritative record, or only history that could be assimilated into it?
  8. Did later neurological reclassification of the foot territory ever trigger review of the founding partition?
  9. Did the 2025 complaint investigation reconstruct the 2017 information state independently, or evaluate the coherence of the accumulated record?
  10. What evidence did SIMT use to conclude “no deficit,” and did it specifically test the feet–movement relationship and the alleged pre-closure notice?
  11. Why was the later dystonia diagnosis considered capable of changing present clinical knowledge but incapable of reopening the historical governance conclusion?
  12. What would count, institutionally, as sufficient evidence that the original decision was not adequately justified?
  13. If no conceivable later fact can answer that question, has the decision become institutionally unfalsifiable?

The analytical task is now precise:

reconstruct the chronology by which a provisional clinical allocation may have become an increasingly protected epistemic fact, and determine whether that protection was earned by inquiry or produced by explanation.
