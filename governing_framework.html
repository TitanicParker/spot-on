01 · Governing framework

The Governing Analytical Framework

A third-pass reconstruction centred on Priorness, the 2017 documentary phase change, dual continuity, corrigibility and the questions that remain capable of changing the model.

Personal analytical edition. The clinical record and original sources remain factually controlling. The pre-discharge Protest is treated as a fixed premise for this personal reconstruction; external proof is a separate question.
Contents

1. Why this case has to be read differently

Most negligence chronologies can be read as a line: referral → consultations → tests → treatment → last note. This case has an additional structural event. The July 2017 specialist synthesis did not merely sit somewhere in the line. It changed the kind of object the case was.

Before that synthesis there was an open information state: painful and structurally abnormal feet, abnormal gait, escalating neurological signs, toe clawing, increased tone, severe young-onset Parkinsonism, and a directly observed lower-limb posture described as dystonic-looking. Several explanations were possible and the relationship among them remained clinically important.

After the synthesis there was a travelling information state: a specialist-authored account that organised those observations into branches, gave them explanatory weight, assigned likely future owners, and became the starting point for later clinicians.

Phase change: the case moves from an open clinical problem that can still be organised in several ways to an authoritative documentary model that later care is likely to inherit. The question is not whether the summary contained intelligent reasoning. It plainly did. The question is whether the authority acquired by the final model was proportionate to the relational inquiry that had actually been completed.

That phase change is the centre of this repository.

2. Priorness: what existed before authority

Priorness is not a claim that earlier evidence is always more accurate. It is a rule about dependency. To understand how a later model was created, begin with material that existed before that model could influence subsequent description.

The pre-summary state includes bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait; genuine structural/cutaneous foot pathology; progressive initiation difficulty and stiffness; increased tone and brisk reflexes; toe clawing and worsening dexterity; severe functional deterioration; an open GP formulation of “parkinsonian or other”; specialist confirmation of severe Parkinsonism; direct specialist observation of a lower-limb posture “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP”; and explicit consideration of very-young-onset and genetic Parkinsonism, including PRKN/PARK2.

The hospital therefore did not receive a case already divided into “Parkinson’s disease” and “unrelated feet.” It received an integrated feet–gait–tone–movement problem and then organised it.

Priorness tells us where to begin because the final synthesis cannot be used to authenticate the adequacy of the process that created it.

3. The unresolved relationship

Two things were simultaneously true. The feet had real structural disease, so Podiatry was legitimate. The lower-limb movement disorder was also real, and Neurology itself recorded dystonic-looking phenomenology in the same founding admission.

The bounded clinical question was therefore not “feet or Parkinsonism?” It was:

What was the relationship between the painful/structurally abnormal feet and the dystonic-looking lower-limb movement?

A mixed answer was always possible. Structural pathology and neurological posture/loading can coexist. A peripheral process can coexist with dystonia. The case never required a single-cause theory.

Phenomenological difference can be genuine without proving biological independence.

A patient can accurately say that pain and tension feel different while both remain parts of an interacting clinical system.

4. The Protest as a pre-closure correction signal

For this personal reconstruction, the pre-discharge Protest is treated as a fixed premise: before final closure, while the patient remained in care and corrective options remained, the responsible consultant was directly told that the problem which brought the patient into care had not been adequately addressed.

That does not prove that the clinician was wrong. It changes the burden of reconstruction.

pre-notice provisional reasoning → direct corrective notice → remaining opportunity → post-notice authoritative finalisation.

The patient did not have to diagnose dystonia, PRKN disease or neuropathy. The informational content of the Protest was simpler: the episode was being completed while the originating problem still felt clinically unresolved.

That creates a decision fork. Proportionate responses could have included re-examination, explicit relational formulation, colleague or movement-disorders input, a defined neurological question to Podiatry, preservation of uncertainty, documentation of disagreement, or a clear return condition. The issue is not that the entire case had to be solved before discharge.

The issue is whether the authority and durability of the disposition became greater than the relational work visibly supporting it.

5. Premature documentary closure

The working hypothesis is not “the diagnosis was wrong.” It is narrower.

A still-contestable relationship may have been converted into an apparently settled travelling information state before the available record demonstrates a proportionate post-notice reassessment, resolution, or explicit preservation of uncertainty.

This is what “premature documentary closure” means here. It can occur even when individual sentences are plausible, even where the overall diagnostic achievement is impressive, and without dishonesty.

The possible mechanism is:

open relational problem → plausible separation hypothesis → direct challenge → final synthesis gives the separation operational authority → later care inherits the result.

The hypothesis is falsifiable. A documented targeted reassessment after notice, a pre-notice source establishing the same patient-attributed separation, or expert evidence that no further relational inquiry was reasonably required could materially weaken it.

6. The discharge synthesis as a phase-changing instrument

The July synthesis did more than list observations. It organised them.

One branch became highly developed:

Parkinsonism → very young onset → genetic differential → PRKN/PARK2 and other genes → imaging → prognosis → treatment strategy.

The foot territory received another route:

corns/calluses/pain/burning → “leg symptoms” → possibly completely separate → possible peripheral neuropathy → possible alcohol contribution → Podiatry.

Every component of the second route may be medically discussable. The question is what the route did. It made it possible for the feet to acquire a future outside the movement-disorder question.

The unresolved bridge received less visible development than either branch on its own. Once the summary travelled, later clinicians did not meet only the raw patient. They met:

patient + specialist summary + categories already allocated.

7. Patient-attributed material and provenance

The final synthesis contains strong formulations attributed to the patient: that pain was “out of the picture,” that phenomena were “not the same problem,” and that he had “markedly divorced” them.

Where an earlier independent source does not authenticate materially equivalent wording, they remain patient-attributed propositions inside the specialist narrative, not free-standing patient evidence.

This matters because, on the Protest premise, the final artefact preserves patient-attributed material supporting separation while not visibly carrying the patient's contrary pre-closure correction signal.

confirmatory patient material travels; disconfirmatory patient material does not travel with equivalent force.

That is a documentary effect. It does not by itself establish motive.

8. Dual continuity: the record may forget; the patient need not

After the Protest, two histories can continue at once.

Institutional continuity: the objection does not travel in the authoritative record, so later clinicians encounter an apparently settled allocation.

Patient continuity: the objection was actually made, the unresolved problem remained unresolved from the patient's perspective, and subsequent care was experienced against that continuing knowledge.

Documentary discontinuity is not experiential discontinuity.

A later “misalignment,” complaint or renewed objection is therefore not automatically a newly invented disagreement simply because the earlier chart did not preserve it. Clinical cooperation is also not epistemic surrender.

9. Later records: stress tests, not automatic corroboration

After the phase change, repetition has to be classified by provenance. A later note can contain a new independent observation, an inherited proposition, a repetition of inherited history, a reinterpretation of new evidence through the inherited model, or genuine reopening of the founding allocation.

Repetition is not independent corroboration merely because it appears in many notes.

The later record is most useful as a sequence of stress tests: what happened when new facts pressed against the old allocation?

10. Boundary-crossing evidence

Several later observations cross the original feet–movement boundary without, by themselves, proving a single mechanism.

  • 2018: withdrawal of procyclidine is followed by rapid return/change in the disputed foot territory and worsening movement/tension.
  • 2020: a foot-drop-like lower-limb phenomenon is directly judged “actually parkinsonian” because power is normal.
  • 2022: the feet show a prolonged morning “warm-up.”
  • 2023: cramping is considered OFF dystonia; leg pain and shuffling are assessed as OFF-related; the clinician records a “misalignment” and acknowledges the first symptoms were in the feet.
  • 2024: pregabalin improves much of the pain but does not remove toe curling.
  • 2025: dopaminergic treatment materially improves longstanding foot sensations/pain.

None of these proves that every historical foot symptom was dystonic. Their value is different: each asks whether the founding boundary remained epistemically secure.

11. Local adaptability versus foundational corrigibility

The longitudinal service was not clinically inert. It changed medication, adjusted doses, investigated alternatives, repeated imaging, considered dystonia, tested Fabry disease, treated neuropathic pain, introduced levodopa and later accepted substantial dopaminergic benefit.

That is real local adaptability.

The separate question is whether any of those developments caused the epistemic status of the founding allocation to change.

The recurrent pattern is:

new fact → local explanation/treatment adjustment → founding allocation remains available.

The record demonstrates high local clinical adaptability, but comparatively little visible reconstruction of the founding feet–movement allocation inside the originating system.

External referral eventually produced genuine global clinical reopening. That is important counter-evidence against any claim of absolute rigidity.

12. The PRKN endpoint and the long pre-Parkinsonian interval

Later independent genetics confirmed pathogenic PRKN/Parkin variants, and external specialist interpretation integrated Parkinsonism and dystonia within the same disease.

This is not a time machine. It does not establish what clinicians were required to know molecularly in 2017. It does not prove that every corn, callus or painful episode was dystonic.

Its legitimate retrospective force is narrower: dystonia was biologically part of the disease actually present; lower-limb dystonia is compatible with PRKN disease; and such dystonia can precede fuller Parkinsonism or remain relatively isolated for years.

That last point matters to the chronology. A long history of lower-limb/foot phenomena does not, by duration alone, prove independence from the later Parkinsonian syndrome.

The contemporary GP “mini-presentation” therefore matters: young age, toe clawing, abnormal gait, increased tone and initiation difficulty were already present before the specialist synthesis; Neurology then explicitly recognised dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology.

Later biology does not create that question. It shows that the question was biologically real.

13. The levodopa asymmetry

The 2017 synthesis is unusually explicit about the decision to delay levodopa. It records rationale, age-related risk, long-term strategy, patient understanding, future treatment expectations and even the possibility of future regret.

That is excellent prospective documentation of a consequential treatment decision.

By contrast, the visible record provides much less transparent evidence of the post-notice clinical work by which the originating foot problem was separated from the directly observed dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenon.

This creates documentary asymmetry in defensibility.

When a later complaint includes “why was dopaminergic treatment not used earlier?”, the record already contains a detailed answer. The more difficult founding question—what happened after direct notice before closure?—has no equally transparent documentary bridge.

The asymmetry is observable. Whether it was consciously designed is a separate mental-state question.

14. Question migration

Over time, the dispute can change questions without anyone necessarily intending to evade the original one.

  • Founding question: What happened after direct notice before closure?
  • Treatment question: Why was levodopa delayed or limited?
  • Defence question: Was longitudinal management reasonable within the model?
  • Governance question: Were treatment decisions appropriate and alternatives considered?
  • Data-governance question: Does the record accurately preserve the historical professional opinion?
  • Finality question: Does a later diagnosis require completed governance decisions to reopen?

Every later question can be legitimate. But answering them does not automatically answer the first.

This is question migration: the subject of review moves toward questions the accumulated record is better equipped to answer.

15. The originating clinician has a different epistemic position

A later clinician may innocently know only what the chart contains.

The originating clinician is different if he personally participated in a material pre-closure event the chart does not contain. He cannot wholly “inherit” his own record as though he were merely another downstream reader.

He may continue to hold the same clinical conclusion. But when later explaining, transmitting or defending that conclusion, his epistemic basis includes both the documentary history and his own direct knowledge of the omitted event.

The hard question is therefore not simply whether the later defence is clinically plausible. It is whether it accurately represents the provenance and completeness of the resolution the clinician personally helped create.

16. Governance and recursive validation

Governance is not automatically independent merely because it occurs later or at a higher level.

A dependency chain can arise:

2017 synthesis → later care interpreted through it → later notes used to demonstrate ongoing reassessment → original clinician relies on those notes in defence → governance reviews accumulated record and defence → governance conclusion adds institutional authority.

This does not make the governance outcome wrong. It creates a question of evidential independence.

A genuinely corrective review should be capable of reconstructing:

pre-summary sources → observations → Protest → post-notice response → finalisation, rather than relying only on the coherence of the mature record.

17. Historical preservation versus epistemic correction

A historical record can accurately preserve that a clinician held a particular opinion even if later evidence changes how that opinion should now be understood.

Those are different truth conditions: was the clinical proposition sufficiently supported; was the opinion reasonable at the time; and is it historically accurate that the opinion was recorded?

Preserving the third does not necessarily answer the first two.

Historical integrity and correction are compatible. An old record can remain intact while later evidence qualifies its present epistemic status.

What mechanism allows new evidence to change the authority of the historical information state without erasing the historical record?

18. Function before motive

The documentary architecture can be analysed before anyone's private intention is known.

Observable questions include what information travelled, what information did not, which decisions were documented in defensible detail, which relational questions were left less transparent, what later evidence was absorbed locally, and what later reviewers actually tested.

Only after those are established should the analysis move to knowledge, appreciation and intention.

functional effect → knowledge-state inference → intentional selectivity → concealment/falsification.

The present documentary case is strongest at the first level. Higher levels require affirmative evidence, particularly native provenance and production history.

19. The theory must be able to lose

Evidence that could materially weaken the central theory includes a targeted post-Protest reassessment; contemporaneous reasoning explaining why the dystonic-looking phenomenon was unrelated to the historical foot presentation; an earlier independent source supporting the strong patient-attributed separation language; proof that the relevant relational formulation was complete before notice and did not gain strength after it; evidence that later clinicians explicitly reconstructed and revised the founding allocation; a PAR/SIMT evidence pack showing independent reconstruction of the pre-closure state; or expert evidence that no further relational inquiry was reasonably required after the notice.

The repository must allow such evidence to narrow or defeat its own theory.

20. The controlling formulation

The mature case theory is not “Parkinson's was missed.” Parkinsonism was genuinely recognised, and PRKN/PARK2 was perceptively considered.

It is not “the feet were all dystonia.” Structural disease was real and mixed mechanisms remain plausible.

It is not “nothing was reconsidered.” The later record is clinically active and adaptive.

A plausible provisional allocation may have acquired greater documentary definiteness and downstream authority than the presently demonstrated post-notice clinical process can independently justify. Once that phase change occurred, later care repeatedly adapted to new facts without clearly reconstructing the founding allocation; later defence and governance then evaluated an accumulated record whose coherence was partly inherited from that founding state.

The irreducible question is:

What was allowed to change what?
