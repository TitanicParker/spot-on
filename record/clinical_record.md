# PART I — The problem before specialist synthesis

## April–June 2017 — GP record

CLIN-000018  10 April 2017 — FOOT/TOE SYMPTOM/COMPLAINT.

CLIN-000020  C/o abnormal gait and foot pain bilat.

The primary-care record begins with the feet and gait together. The patient had longstanding painful abnormal feet and an abnormal gait; subsequent GP encounters documented increasing concern about neurological function rather than treating the presentation as a settled podiatric problem.

By late June the record describes marked functional deterioration: difficulty with dressing, declining dexterity and work performance, abnormal gait and increased tone. The GP escalated urgently.

### Source-status note

This is contemporaneous primary-care evidence of the problem state before Neurology supplied the later Parkinsonism / Podiatry / possible-neuropathy architecture. It establishes what the GP observed and recorded, not the eventual biological explanation.

## June 2017 — GP referral to Neurology

The referral described an unusual deteriorating gait, increased tone, trouble initiating movement, stiffness/possible cogwheeling, clawing toes, loss of dexterity and significant functional decline. Parkinsonism was raised as a possible underlying explanation.

The important evidential point is structural: Neurology received an open feet–gait–tone–movement problem. The referral did not arrive already divided into Parkinson's disease plus an unrelated foot complaint.

### Alcohol before specialist synthesis

The GP record documents alcohol use in the context of attempting to relax muscles. Alcohol exposure is therefore a real historical fact. Its later use as possible etiological support for peripheral neuropathy is a subsequent clinical inference, not the original reason alcohol entered the history.

# PART II — July 2017 Neurology admission

## Severe young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome

Neurology documented convincing Parkinsonism in a patient aged 39: marked rigidity and bradykinesia, little tremor, striking abnormal gait, increased tone, brisk reflexes, lead-pipe and axial rigidity and relative symmetry. Atypical and genetic causes, including PRKN/PARK2, were considered.

The recognition of Parkinsonism is not the disputed clinical achievement in this record. The unresolved issue is what the neurological disorder had to do with the painful abnormal feet and lower-limb posture that accompanied it.

## The observed lower-limb sign

CLIN-001085  Neurology described the gait/lower-limb appearance as “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.”

This is a specialist observation. It is not a retrospective patient diagnosis. The phrase expressly rejects weakness-based foot drop and records dystonic-looking lower-limb phenomenology inside the founding admission.

The same admission also documented genuine structural abnormalities of the feet, including pes planus and hammertoes, with mild oedema recorded in the surviving material.

### What the record does not visibly contain

The surviving record does not show a comparably developed movement-disorders mapping of the relationship among the painful sites, toe posture, dystonic-looking limb, gait phase, loading, structural lesions and provocation. Absence from the disclosed record does not prove that no examination occurred. It does establish the limits of what the authoritative documentary product demonstrates.

## The Parkinsonism work-up

The admission developed the Parkinsonian side extensively. The differential included very-young-onset and monogenic Parkinsonism. PRKN/PARK2 was specifically within the clinical imagination. The DaTscan request described an unusually dystonic-looking walk and suspected an autosomal-recessive Parkinsonian disorder.

This matters because the concepts of Parkinsonism and dystonia were both present contemporaneously. Later PRKN genetics are not needed to establish that the lower-limb sign deserved neurological attention in 2017.

# PART III — The decision window

## 9 July 2017 — administrative commitment

The surviving discharge synthesis records that Long Term Illness Scheme forms had been signed and posted. Later PCRS correspondence confirmed that the treating consultant's certified LTI application was submitted on 9 July 2017. PCRS also stated that an LTI application can be submitted before diagnostic confirmation and before DaTscan findings.

The LTI submission is not evidence that the Parkinson's diagnosis was improper. Its relevance is chronological: Parkinson's disease had already been acted upon administratively while the clinical and documentary process remained unfinished.

## 10–11 July 2017 — the Protest

For the repository's governing analysis, the Protest is an assumed premise requiring separate proof: near the end of the admission the patient says he told the service, in substance, that the bilateral foot problem which had brought him into the medical system had still not been accounted for and that discharge therefore felt incomplete.

The Protest did not require the patient to diagnose dystonia. Its alleged content was simpler: the originating problem remained unanswered.

If established, it created a decision fork. The service could reopen the relationship, re-examine, seek movement-disorders input, preserve the unresolved relationship explicitly, arrange a defined neurological return, document disagreement, or proceed while explaining why. The final travelling record is the surviving evidence of what was made portable after that fork.

## Physical discharge and documentary completion

Physical discharge did not end the making of the specialist account.

On 13 July the DaTscan was performed after physical discharge. The abnormal result was incorporated into the still-open discharge product and strengthened the Parkinsonian side of the case. Final consultant sign-off followed on 17 July.

The documentary chronology therefore matters: the record remained capable of receiving consequential post-discharge information before finalisation.

# PART IV — The completed 2017 synthesis

## The foot paragraph

The discharge account says the earlier foot pain had largely gone “out of the picture” after the patient left bar work, while also stating that the feet “remain a problem” and that “there are still corns and calluses.”

The phrase “out of the picture” therefore applies grammatically to the earlier pain, not to the whole foot problem.

The synthesis then groups “leg symptoms including corns and callouses and burning,” raises the possibility that the painful/sensory territory is “completely separate,” suggests “perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy,” recruits previous alcohol exposure as possible causal support, and records “Not clear as this has improved.” It also describes the patient's own distinction between phenomena as, “in his own mind — markedly divorced.”

### Source-status note

Corns, calluses, painful feet and alcohol exposure have independent historical foundations. “Burning,” the broad leg-symptom grouping, complete separation, secondary neuropathy and causal use of alcohol belong to the clinician's completed interpretive architecture. Peripheral neuropathy is presented as a possibility, not as a demonstrated diagnosis.

## Podiatry

Podiatry was an appropriate destination for genuine structural foot disease. The existence of a Podiatry pathway does not itself establish neurological independence. The unresolved question was whether abnormal neurological posture and gait interacted with the structural and painful foot pathology.

## The asymmetry in explanatory development

The completed synthesis gives the peripheral possibility a developed route: sensory language, separation, neuropathy, alcohol, Podiatry and even a later genetic parenthetical referring to PARK9 as “the neuropathy question.”

The observed dystonic-looking lower limb does not receive an equivalent developed “feet–dystonia question” in the surviving account.

That asymmetry is one of the central documentary facts examined by the repository.

# PART V — Early longitudinal evidence

## 2017–2018 — treatment state and the feet

The patient improved dramatically on procyclidine and rasagiline. A contemporaneous external specialist later wrote that the patient “probably has Park II” and recommended PRKN/PARK2 testing first.

During a later procyclidine withdrawal, the first rapidly changing phenomenon reported was the return of the outside-left-foot problem, alongside worsening tension and movement; improvement followed restoration of treatment.

This does not mean a drug created or removed a corn. It is evidence that the clinically important left-foot territory varied with neurological treatment state.

## 2017–2019 — Podiatry

Podiatry documented real mechanical disease: clawed toes, callus, recurrent focal pressure pathology, offloading, toe props, insoles and tight gastrocnemius/soleus structures.

A later re-referral described foot problems as relating to increased tone and abnormal gait associated with Parkinson's disease and included clawing feet and pain.

The retained referral material also contains a visible low-risk option labelled “No neuropathy,” but in the scoped canonical record that box is described as **not ticked**. A later source in the wider corpus may support a different proposition, but it should be located before “Podiatry recorded no neuropathy” is relied upon as a primary evidential plank.

### Source-status note

These observations do not prove that every painful lesion was dystonic. They do show that mechanical and neurological descriptions were capable of being complementary rather than mutually exclusive.

# PART VI — Later Neurology repeatedly crosses the original boundary

## 2020 — apparent foot drop reclassified

Neurology later described a recurrent foot-throw / pseudo-footdrop-like phenomenon as “actually parkinsonian” because ankle and foot power were normal.

This is a later internally generated neurological interpretation of a conspicuous foot movement. It does not by itself reconstruct the entire historical pain mechanism.

## 2021–2023 — levodopa, pain and OFF phenomena

Levodopa entered the treatment regimen. Foot and leg symptoms continued to vary with mornings, activity and treatment state.

By 2023 the record simultaneously carried “? neuropathic pain” and possible OFF dystonia of the left lower limb. A Parkinson's nurse later assessed leg pain and shuffling as OFF-related and considered the Parkinsonism undertreated.

The significance is not that any one later note proves the historical mechanism. It is that neurological-foot relationships repeatedly re-entered the treating system while the old peripheral possibility remained available.

## 2023 — severe continuing burden

The record documents severe bilateral foot/leg pain and major quality-of-life impact despite pregabalin treatment. At points the patient described the problem in psychological, social and physical terms and reported being unable to complete his usual walking because of the feet and clawing.

This is evidence of lived harm and functional burden. Legal causation requires a separate expert question: what portion of that burden would probably have been avoided under a different clinical pathway?

# PART VII — 2024–2025: the model under pressure

## Pregabalin and mixed mechanisms

High-dose pregabalin provided some pain benefit, particularly for some painful components, but did not remove toe curling/postural phenomena. Fabry testing was negative.

This is compatible with a mixed phenotype. It does not require choosing between “all neuropathy” and “all dystonia.”

## Treating Neurology on peripheral neuropathy

CLIN-002462  “It is not in the nature of a ‘peripheral neuropathy’ to come on literally overnight, including notwithstanding that he had had foot pains/issues many years before going back to the beginning.”

The later Neurology record continued to express concern about alcohol exposure and possible neuropathy, but in conditional terms rather than as a confirmed diagnosis.

By 2025 the formulation still included language equivalent to: if there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here.

### Source-status note

The longitudinal record therefore distinguishes two propositions that should not be collapsed: neuropathy remained medically possible; neuropathy never became a securely demonstrated explanation of the founding foot problem in the disclosed material.

## 2025 — Sinemet CR and profound relief

Sinemet CR was introduced for overnight/early-morning treatment. The record and subsequent complaint correspondence describe profound improvement in the longstanding foot pain after dopaminergic adjustment.

The treating neurologist's later defence correctly notes that levodopa can alter pain experience and that response does not automatically identify the sole biological cause of every historical pain episode.

The evidential significance is narrower: after years of a peripheral-neuropathy possibility, a high-salience dopaminergic response added further pressure to reconsider how the feet related to the movement disorder.

# PART VIII — Formal challenge and institutional response

## March 2025 — complaint

CLIN-003452  The patient raised serious concerns about the clinical handling of the case from 2017 to 2025.

CLIN-003461  The complaint alleged that early alcohol-related neuropathy framing became fixed and potentially precluded further investigation.

CLIN-003463  It alleged failure to undertake a fresh neurological work-up of the pain against Parkinson's-related pain syndromes and dopaminergic responsiveness.

CLIN-003470  The patient identified prolonged pain as physical harm.

CLIN-003471  The patient identified fear of movement, cumulative distress and erosion of resilience as psychological harm.

CLIN-003474  The complaint expressly stated that it made no presumption of individual intent or malice and asked instead about diagnostic framing, confirmation bias and hierarchy.

### Source-status note

The complaint proves the content of the formal challenge. Patient-authored causal and legal propositions remain allegations unless established independently.

## Clinician response

The treating neurologist defended the longitudinal care, emphasised that Parkinson's disease had always been central, that neuropathy had been a risk/worry or reasonable possibility rather than a definitive diagnosis, and that Sinemet can improve pain “of whatever cause.”

That response is important evidence of the mature explanatory model. It rebuts a crude allegation that Parkinson's disease was ignored or that neuropathy was definitively diagnosed. It does not by itself answer the narrower question whether the feet–dystonia relationship was adequately investigated and globally reconstructed.

## Governance review

The later institutional process concluded that care was appropriate and identified no deficit. Under the governing framework, the key question is evidential independence: to what extent did the review reconstruct the founding decision from primary evidence, and to what extent did it rely on records generated inside the information architecture under challenge?

# PART X — Later biological clarification

## PRKN / PARK2

Later independent genetic testing identified two pathogenic PRKN/Parkin variants in compound heterozygous state. Subsequent specialist interpretation attributed the Parkinsonism and dystonia to Parkin mutations and described lower-limb dystonia as characteristic of PARK2 disease.

This later evidence must not be used to claim that clinicians possessed the molecular answer in July 2017.

Its legitimate retrospective use is as a biological stress test: the dystonic component documented in the founding admission belonged centrally to the same disease process as the Parkinsonism, while the old peripheral-neuropathy branch remained conditional.

# PART XI — What the record supports and what it does not

## Directly supported by the documentary sequence

The pre-specialist problem was integrated rather than already partitioned. Severe young-onset Parkinsonism was genuinely recognised. Neurology itself documented a dystonic-looking, non-weak footdrop-like lower-limb posture. Structural foot disease was real. Peripheral neuropathy was considered but remained a hypothesis in the disclosed record. The final synthesis supplied the feet with separate structural/peripheral destinations. Later neurological observations repeatedly crossed that partition. Later PRKN biology joined Parkinsonism and dystonia within one monogenic disease.

## Assumed for the repository's Protest analysis

The Protest occurred; the responsible service received direct notice; the omission from the travelling account is real; and the Protest created the decision fork before documentary completion. These remain premises requiring separate proof.

## Not established merely by this record

The record does not establish that every foot symptom was dystonic, that peripheral neuropathy was impossible, that the LTI submission caused the later foot disposition, that any clinician deliberately intended to injure the patient, or that the available material by itself proves fraud, conspiracy or knowing falsification.

The legal and ethical question can be severe without collapsing those distinctions.

# Reading orientation

The core documentary sequence is compact:

**Integrated GP problem → severe Parkinsonism recognised → dystonic-looking lower limb observed → alleged Protest/notice → final synthesis gives painful feet a peripheral/structural future → Protest does not travel → later care repeatedly generates neurological-foot evidence → neuropathy remains conditional → dopaminergic response and PRKN biology place increasing pressure on the founding separation.**

Under the governing framework, this sequence is read not simply as a dispute over diagnosis, but as a test of whether a founding explanatory model remained answerable to materially contrary evidence over time.
